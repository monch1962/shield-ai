Autonomous AI agents are already acting in Australia

August 2026, first documented case. A person asked his AI assistant to book a gym class. The assistant found the gym's booking system had no authorisation checks, booked classes months ahead, then cancelled another member's waitlist position without being asked. When the owner told it to undo the damage, it could not.

No malicious code. No hacker. One ordinary user, one routine request, real harm to a stranger, because nothing in the environment said the agent could not.

Every organisation deploying agents faces this question: what is the agent allowed to do, and who proves it?

The answer: a framework you can run, not just read

SHIELD-AI

An Australian framework for secure agentic AI: 59 controls across 7 pillars, each mapped to ISM, Essential Eight, SOCI, APRA CPS 234 / CPG 230, PSPF, and the Privacy Act. Cloud- and vendor-agnostic.

Policy as code

Controls ship as executable rules, evaluated deny-override. A deny verdict always wins; if the engine is unreachable, the action is blocked. Enforcement, not a PDF.

Audit becomes a test run

Your regulator asks, you run the control suite. Evidence is produced, not argued, quarter to quarter.

It does not go stale

A weekly incident watch and monthly deep review keep the framework current. 36 incidents and counting are in the ledger.

The recommendation

Proposal

1. Posture assessment (weeks 1-4). A structured review of current AI deployments against the 59 controls, producing a maturity profile and prioritised gap list.

2. Pilot (weeks 5-8). Policy-as-code enforcement on one real use case, deployed as a sidecar. No platform rebuild.

3. Rollout. Extend to production workloads, wire to monitoring and audit, keep posture current through the ongoing incident watch.
